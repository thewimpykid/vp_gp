================================================================================
  NQ STACKED VOLUME PROFILE — HOW IT WORKS
  intuitiveVP / stacked_vp.py
  Last updated: 2026-06-09
================================================================================

This system identifies high-probability reversal zones on NQ futures (Nasdaq-100
E-mini) using multi-timeframe volume profile analysis combined with structural
price levels. It runs entirely on prior data — zero look-ahead. Zones computed
before market open reflect only what a live trader would have known that morning.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. DATA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Source: 1Min_NQ.csv — Sierra Chart NQ 1-minute OHLCV export, 2014–2026.
        4.17 million bars. Cross-validated against Yahoo Finance NQ=F (0.997 corr).
Format: semicolon-separated, European number notation (3.575,75 = 3575.75 NQ pts).
Cache:  On first load, parsed to Parquet for fast subsequent reads (<1s vs ~30s).

Working range used: 2020–2026. Pre-2020 excluded to keep VP windows representative
of current NQ volatility regime (~200–400 pt daily range vs ~100 pt pre-2018).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  2. VOLUME PROFILE CONSTRUCTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A Volume Profile answers: "At each price level, how much volume was traded?"

NQ 1-minute bars give OHLCV but not individual trade prices. The system uses the
standard OHLCV approximation: distribute each bar's volume uniformly across all
5-point bins that span its [Low, High] range.

Example: a bar with Low=21000, High=21025, Volume=500
  → spans bins 21000, 21005, 21010, 21015, 21020, 21025 (6 bins)
  → each bin receives 500 / 6 = 83.3 contracts

This is done vectorised across all bars in the window. The result is a histogram
of volume-at-price with 5-point resolution.

From the histogram three key levels are extracted:

  POC (Point of Control):
    The single 5-pt bin with the highest volume. This is where the market spent
    the most time and traded the most — the "fair value" anchor of the session.
    Weight: 1.1

  VAH / VAL (Value Area High / Low):
    The price range containing 70% of total volume, constructed greedily by
    adding the highest-volume bins one at a time until the 70% threshold is met.
    VAH = top of the highest VA bin. VAL = bottom of the lowest VA bin.
    Price inside the VA is "accepted" by the market. Price outside tends to
    reject and return toward VA — the core mean-reversion logic.
    Weight: 1.2 each

  HVN (High Volume Node):
    Peaks in the volume histogram with prominence ≥ 35% of the peak bar's volume
    (HVN_PROMINENCE = 0.35). HVNs represent price ranges where the market
    clustered heavily — strong support/resistance because unfilled orders remain.
    HVN boundaries (edges) are the actionable levels: price decelerates and
    reverses at HVN edges, not at the HVN center.
    Detection: scipy.signal.find_peaks on a Savitzky-Golay smoothed profile.

  LVN (Low Volume Node):
    Valleys in the volume histogram where volume < 40% of mean volume
    (LVN_DEPTH = 0.40). LVNs are price gaps or thin zones — price travels
    quickly through them in trending moves but can also act as magnets when
    approached from the other side.
    Weight: 0.9

  Shelf / Ledge (DISABLED):
    Originally: classify HVN boundaries by their slope.
      Shelf = gradual taper (avg_slope ≤ 0.08)
      Ledge = abrupt cliff (avg_slope ≥ 0.25)
    Disabled after backtesting showed shelf/ledge solo reversal rates (6.5%)
    were below the random baseline (11.9%). Including them added noise.
    INCLUDE_SHELF = False, INCLUDE_LEDGE = False.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  3. THREE VOLUME PROFILE TIMEFRAMES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Three separate VP windows are computed simultaneously. Each window uses a
different amount of prior history, capturing structure at different time scales.

  30d  (~21 trading days, 1 month):
    Short-term structure — recent consolidation zones, fresh POCs, current value
    area. Most responsive to recent price action. Levels shift week-to-week.

  60d  (~42 trading days, 2 months):
    Medium-term structure — captures a full market cycle (most NQ swings are
    20–40 trading days). VAH/VAL represent broader acceptance ranges.

  90d  (~63 trading days, 3 months):
    Long-term structural floor/ceiling — quarterly value area. Slow to move.
    When price revisits a 90d HVN from months prior it often gets a large reaction.

Weekly was tested and removed — it added noise without improving reversal rates.

Mode options:
  Rolling (default):  window = last N trading days from df tail
  Anchored:           window = calendar boundary (1st of month, Monday of week)
  Anchored is used for sample-day charts (better for intraday analysis since
  it aligns with how market participants think about monthly/weekly ranges).

The three profiles are computed independently. Each contributes its own set of
VAH, VAL, POC, HVN-edge, and LVN features. Features from all three then enter
the confluence stacking step.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  4. STRUCTURAL PRICE LEVEL FEATURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Beyond volume profiles, six categories of structural levels are added:

4a. PDH / PDL — Prior Day High / Low
    Weight: 1.4 (highest among all structural features)
    Source: daily OHLC aggregated from 1m bars, last 10 prior sessions.
    Why: PDH/PDL are the single most-watched NQ levels. Institutional desks
    explicitly reference prior day extremes for entry/exit. When price sweeps
    PDH intraday and reverses, that's a textbook SFP (Stop Fill Play).
    Per-day timeframe IDs: d-1 (yesterday), d-2 (day before), d-3... d-10.
    This is critical: if yesterday's high and 3 days ago's high are both near
    21500, they get unique timeframe IDs (d-1 and d-3), so when they stack they
    boost n_tf count exponentially (n_tf² in the scoring formula). Two days
    stacking at the same price doubles the exponent term.

4b. PWH / PWL — Prior Week High / Low
    Weight: 1.2
    Source: ISO week aggregation, prior completed week only.
    Why: weekly extremes are headline levels for swing traders and options desks.
    NQ weekly options expiry creates demand for strikes near PWH/PWL.

4c. IBH / IBL — Initial Balance High / Low
    Weight: 1.6 (highest weight of any single feature)
    Source: 09:30–10:30 ET (minutes 570–630) for each of 3 prior sessions.
    Why: the Initial Balance is the first hour of regular trading. IB extremes
    define the "auction range" that day. Research (Market Profile theory) shows:
    - ~70% of days stay within 2× IB range
    - IB extensions (breakouts above IBH or below IBL) are high-probability
      continuation moves
    - Failed IB extensions (reversal back through IB after a sweep) are among
      the cleanest reversal setups in intraday trading
    Timeframe IDs: d-1, d-2, d-3 (same per-day ID system as PDH/PDL).
    Weight 1.6 reflects that IBH/IBL backtested with the highest solo reversal
    rate of any session-derived level.

4d. ONH / ONL — Overnight High / Low
    Weight: 1.2
    Source: 00:00–09:29 ET (pre-market session) for each of 3 prior sessions.
    Why: overnight range defines the "reference" for the coming RTH session.
    Price opening outside the overnight range signals institutional interest.
    Gap fills (price returning to the prior overnight close) are systematic in NQ.

4e. Gap Level — Prior RTH Close
    Weight: 1.1
    Source: close price of the last RTH bar (09:30–16:15 ET) for each of 3 prior
    sessions. This is the gap reference: if NQ opens above yesterday's RTH close,
    there's an open gap below. Gap fills are common in NQ (estimated 60–70%
    frequency) making prior RTH close a reliable attractor level.

4f. PMH / PML — Prior Month High / Low
    Weight: 1.3
    Source: calendar month aggregation, prior completed month only.
    Why: monthly extremes are macro-level reference points. Large traders use
    monthly ranges for position sizing. When price tests PMH/PML, it often gets
    a multi-hour reaction.

4g. Round Numbers — Psychological Levels
    Weight: 1.5 for 500-point levels (e.g. 21000, 21500, 22000)
             1.0 for 100-point levels (e.g. 21100, 21200, 21300)
    Range: within ±4 × 20-day ATR of current price.
    Why: round numbers attract market attention because options strikes cluster
    there, stop losses cluster there, and retail traders anchor to them. At NQ
    21500 vs 21473, the former always gets more attention. The psychological
    effect is documented in order-book data (higher cancel-replace activity near
    round numbers as algos test liquidity).
    500-point levels carry higher weight because NQ futures have standard contract
    rollover stops and large-lot hedges placed at 500pt increments.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  5. CONFLUENCE STACKING (The Core Logic)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

All features from all timeframes are now pooled: VP VAH/VAL/POC/HVN/LVN from
30d/60d/90d profiles, PDH/PDL/PWH/PWL, IBH/IBL/ONH/ONL/gap, PMH/PML, rounds.

The key insight: a price level with evidence from multiple independent sources
is far more significant than one from a single source. If the 30d VAH, the
90d HVN upper boundary, yesterday's high, and a 500pt round number all land
within 15 points of each other — that is a high-conviction structural level.

Stacking algorithm (greedy clustering):

  1. Sort all features by price ascending.
  2. Scan left to right. For each unassigned feature, grab all other features
     within ±tolerance of it. Tolerance = 3.0 × 5.0 pt bin = 15 points.
  3. All grabbed features form a cluster. The cluster's price is the mean
     of all member feature prices.
  4. Count unique timeframes contributing (n_tf). Two features from the same
     timeframe count as n_tf = 1.
  5. Score = n_tf² × Σ(feature weights)

The n_tf² exponent is the key design choice. It makes multi-timeframe alignment
exponentially more valuable than single-timeframe concentration:

  1 timeframe, total weight 4.0 → score = 1² × 4.0 = 4.0
  2 timeframes, total weight 4.0 → score = 2² × 4.0 = 16.0
  3 timeframes, total weight 4.0 → score = 3² × 4.0 = 36.0
  4 timeframes, total weight 4.0 → score = 4² × 4.0 = 64.0

This means a zone where 30d VAH, 60d POC, PDH d-1, and IBH all agree is 16×
more confident than the same weight concentrated in one timeframe.

Per-day timeframe IDs for PDH/PDL and IBH/IBL amplify this further: if the
last three days all made their high near 21500, those are d-1, d-2, and d-3 —
three separate timeframes all contributing. The result is a very high n_tf count
even from daily features alone, correctly rewarding price levels where multiple
sessions found the same structural barrier.

Final output: zones sorted by score descending. Score range typically 4–120+.
Zones scoring 3+ unique timeframes are considered "high confidence."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  6. ZONE SELECTION FOR DAILY OUTPUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

From all scored zones, 5 are selected for display per day:

  Step 1: Filter to n_tf ≥ 2 (drop single-timeframe noise). If fewer than 4
          remain, fall back to all zones regardless of n_tf count.

  Step 2: Expand ATR multiplier until ≥ 5 qualifying zones are within range.
          Start at ±2.0 ATR from current close, expand by 0.5 each try up to
          ±5.0 ATR. 20-day ATR ≈ 200–350 pts on recent NQ.

  Step 3: Balance: take ceil(5/2)=3 from above current price, 2 from below.
          Fill deficit from the other side if one side is short.
          This ensures zones both above AND below price are always present —
          avoids the failure mode where all zones cluster on one side.

  Step 4: Sort selected zones by price ascending for display.

Chart display: each zone rendered as a band centered on zone.price.
  Band half-width = zone.price × 0.0005 (0.05% of price, ~11 points at NQ 22000).
  This scales proportionally with NQ's level rather than being a fixed point count.
  Color: magenta colormap, darker = higher score.
  Dashed line at zone center price.
  Label: price, [R/S] role, feature types, n_tf count.
  Gold dotted lines: actual HOD and LOD for the session (post-hoc reference only).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  7. SCORING AND WEIGHTS SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Feature type            Weight    Rationale
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
ibh / ibl               1.6       Strongest intraday barrier in Market Profile
pdh / pdl               1.4       Most-watched NQ level; institutional orders
pml / pmh               1.3       Monthly extremes, macro reference
vah / val               1.2       70% value area boundaries (Market Profile)
onh / onl               1.2       Overnight range extremes, gap analysis
pwh / pwl               1.2       Weekly extremes, options/weekly reference
round (500pt)           1.5       Highest psycho level; options strike density
round (100pt)           1.0       Standard psycho level, retail stop clustering
poc                     1.1       Highest-volume bin; fair value anchor
lvn                     0.9       Thin zone; fast travel or reversal attractor
shelf (DISABLED)        0.8       Gradual HVN boundary — disabled (below baseline)
ledge (DISABLED)        1.3       Abrupt HVN boundary — disabled (below baseline)

Final zone score = n_tf² × Σ(weights of all member features)

Example — a zone at 21500 with:
  30d VAH (1.2) + 60d HVN upper (ledge disabled, ignored) + PDH d-1 (1.4) +
  IBH d-2 (1.6) + round 500pt (1.5) + 90d POC (1.1)
  = 5 unique timeframes (30d, 60d, d-1, d-2, psychological)
  weights sum = 1.2 + 1.4 + 1.6 + 1.5 + 1.1 = 6.8
  score = 5² × 6.8 = 25 × 6.8 = 170.0 — extremely high confidence

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  8. NO LOOK-AHEAD ENFORCEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every sample-day chart and every HOD/LOD coverage test uses strict walk-forward
computation. The pipeline is:

  df_prior = all bars with date < target_day
  df_window = df_prior.tail(30_000)       # ~90 days of 1m bars

  run(df_window, anchored=True) → zones

No data from the target day or any future day enters the VP computation.
The 30,000-bar tail covers all three lookback windows (90d ≈ 35,100 bars at
390 bars/day) plus the structural features that only require last ~10 sessions.

Why 30,000 and not the exact window? Provides a comfortable buffer while keeping
computation time reasonable. 30k bars = ~76 trading days = more than enough for
the 90d VP window.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  9. HOD/LOD COVERAGE VALIDATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The most honest test of the system: does any zone land within ±25 points of the
actual day's High or Low?

Method (walk-forward, 60 sampled days):
  1. Sample 60 days randomly from eligible pool (≥300 bars, ≥90 prior days).
  2. For each day: compute zones from strictly prior data only.
  3. Find minimum distance from any zone to actual HOD and LOD.
  4. Hit if distance ≤ 0.12% of mid-price (≈25 pts at 21000).

Results (seed=42, 60 days from 2020–2026 pool):
  HOD captured: 68.3%   (41/60)
  LOD captured: 70.0%   (42/60)
  Both captured: 53.3%  (32/60)
  Avg distance HOD: ~15 pts
  Avg distance LOD: ~16 pts

Interpretation: on 7 of 10 days, at least one zone is within 25 points of the
session extreme. The ~30% miss rate is concentrated on:
  a) Trend days: price breaks hundreds of points below all prior structure
     (e.g. crash days like 2024-08-01). Correct behavior — no prior structure
     means no zone. Do not fade trend days at arbitrary levels.
  b) Days where NQ gaps far beyond any prior level at open (gap-and-go).
  c) Low-volume holiday sessions with compressed ranges.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  10. WHAT THE SYSTEM IS NOT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Not a mechanical buy/sell signal. Zones are reversal-probability zones, not
entries. A zone showing "RESIST" does not guarantee a reversal — it marks where
the probability of rejection is elevated compared to a random price level.

Not designed for trending days. When NQ opens above prior week high and grinds
higher all day, the system correctly produces no zones in the upper range (no
prior structure). The correct response is to not trade against the trend.

Not calibrated to specific tick targets. The band width (±11 points at NQ 22000)
is for visualization only. Actual entry/exit triggers require additional criteria
(candlestick patterns, delta divergence, volume exhaustion, etc.).

Not guaranteed forward-valid beyond the current vol regime. The 2014–2017 data
showed zero edge because NQ's daily ATR was ~100 pts then vs ~200–350 pts now.
If NQ's ATR compresses below ~150 pts for a sustained period, reassess.
